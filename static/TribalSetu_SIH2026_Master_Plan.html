## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT ANALYSIS

### 1.1 Official Problem Statement (PS ID: 26239)
> *"The implementation of these schemes involves multiple stages, including applicant registration, online application, document submission, eligibility verification, scrutiny, screening, selection, communication, and post-selection/fellowship management. At present, several stages involve manual scrutiny, repeated correspondence and multiple levels of verification, resulting in processing delays, repetitive administrative effort, limited real-time visibility and scope for errors in verification and workflow management."*

### 1.2 Three Scheme Profiles in One Configurable Platform
1. **PMS-ST (Post-Matric Scholarship for ST Students):** A centrally sponsored scheme with State/UT and portal-specific verification steps. Model its applicable state workflow and current academic-year guideline; do not assume a national competitive merit-slot process.
2. **NFST (National Fellowship for Higher Education of ST Students):** The MoTA overview currently describes 750 fresh awards and merit selection based on Master's marks, with stated preferences for girls, Divyangjan, and PVTGs. Post-selection fellowship tracking is a distinct workflow.
3. **NOS (National Overseas Scholarship for ST Students):** The MoTA overview currently describes 20 awards and interview-based merit selection by an expert committee. Overseas admission and award administration require their own documents and post-selection steps.

These details must be checked against the official guideline for each academic year before rules are published. See the [MoTA Scholarship page](https://tribal.nic.in/ScholarshiP.aspx) and the [DBT scheme directory](https://dbttribal.gov.in/AllScheme.aspx). Keep NESTS/EMRS out of the core build unless the team confirms it as an explicit extension to this problem statement.

### 1.3 Problem Areas Addressed by the Prototype
- Repeated manual scrutiny and correspondence can be reduced with visible workflow states, clear deficiency reasons, and applicant resubmission.
- Different scheme rules and documents need a common system with independently configurable workflows.
- OCR and cross-document matching can help officials find missing or inconsistent fields, but uncertainty must remain visible and reviewable.
- Dashboards and audit history can improve status visibility and accountability; savings in time or error rates must be measured rather than assumed.

## 4. MATHEMATICAL & ALGORITHMIC FOUNDATIONS

### 4.1 ELA as a Document-Anomaly Signal
ELA compares an image with a recompressed copy and can highlight regions whose compression behavior differs. This is a heuristic signal, not proof that a document was edited: normal scanning, resizing, messaging-app compression, or repeated saving can also create anomalies, and sophisticated edits may not be detected. Any ELA result must include its limitations and route uncertain cases to human review.

1. **Compression Error Grid:**  
   Given original image $I_{\text{orig}}$ and standard recompressed baseline image $I_{\text{baseline}} = \mathcal{Q}_{90}(I_{\text{orig}})$:
   $$\Delta(x, y) = |I_{\text{orig}}(x, y) - I_{\text{baseline}}(x, y)|$$
2. **Local Patch Variance:**  
   Divide the grayscale difference map $\Delta_{\text{gray}}$ into an $N \times M$ grid of patches $P_{i,j}$ of dimensions $w_p \times h_p$:
   $$\sigma_{i,j} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{w_p h_p} \sum_{x \in P_{i,j}} \sum_{y \in P_{i,j}} (\Delta_{\text{gray}}(x, y) - \mu_{i,j})^2}$$
3. **Anomaly Ratio ($R_{\text{tamper}}$):**
   $$R_{\text{tamper}} = \frac{\max_{i,j}(\sigma_{i,j})}{\frac{1}{N M} \sum_{i,j} \sigma_{i,j} + \epsilon}$$
   - A threshold may be used for a prototype experiment only after it is measured on representative genuine and altered documents. Report it as an anomaly score, never as a mathematical finding of fraud or the sole reason to reject an application.

### 4.2 Keep Eligibility, Authenticity, Selection, and Payment Separate
Do not use one composite trust score to decide eligibility, fraud, merit selection, and payment readiness. These are different decisions with different evidence and accountable owners:

1. **Scheme eligibility:** deterministic checks against the approved scheme rules for the selected academic year. Missing or unreadable evidence is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, not an automatic pass or fail.
2. **Document authenticity signals:** OCR confidence, QR signature result, cross-document mismatch, and ELA anomaly are separate evidence fields. They may prioritize review but do not prove fraud on their own.
3. **Merit and selection:** use only the selected scheme's officially approved selection method. A generic academic/economic/gender/PVTG weighting must not be treated as an official selection formula.
4. **Payment readiness:** DBT or bank-seeding status is a post-selection readiness check. An unseeded account must lead to a correction workflow, not reduce academic merit or imply fraud.
5. **Human decision:** an authorized officer records approve, reject, or request-correction with a reason. AI signals never auto-approve, auto-reject, or trigger a real payment. Applicants need a correction and grievance/appeal path.

For the SIH demo, a non-binding review-priority indicator may be shown, with its inputs and limitations visible. Do not present arbitrary score weights or thresholds as MoTA policy.

### 4.3 Fuzzy Identity Matching Formulation (Token Sort Levenshtein)
Given strings $S_1$ (e.g. Aadhaar name) and $S_2$ (e.g. Caste Certificate name):
1. Preprocess: Lowercase, remove special characters, tokenize into word sets $T_1$ and $T_2$.
2. Sort tokens lexicographically: $T_1^* = \text{sort}(T_1)$, $T_2^* = \text{sort}(T_2)$.
3. Re-join into strings: $S_1^* = \text{join}(T_1^*)$, $S_2^* = \text{join}(T_2^*)$.
4. Compute normalized Levenshtein ratio:
   $$\text{Ratio}(S_1^*, S_2^*) = \left( 1 - \frac{\text{LevenshteinDistance}(S_1^*, S_2^*)}{\max(|S_1^*|, |S_2^*|)} \right) \times 100$$
   - Similarity is a review signal only. Any threshold must be evaluated against representative language and name-variation data; it cannot be a sole rejection rule.

## 5. PRIVACY & SECURITY DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

The controls below are requirements to implement and review, not a statement of current DPDP Act compliance. Masking or hashing Aadhaar alone does not establish compliance. Before processing real applicant data, define and review lawful purpose, clear notice, consent or other applicable legal basis, data minimization, retention/deletion, access and grievance handling, breach response, and security controls. See the [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) and applicable rules.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│          PERSONAL DATA HANDLING DESIGN (NOT A COMPLIANCE CERTIFICATE)    │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                        │
│  [Uploaded Aadhaar Card / Camera Capture]                              │
│                      │                                                 │
│                      ▼                                                 │
│  [Client-Side / In-Memory Ingestion Worker]                            │
│  - Regex / Spatial OCR identifies: 12-Digit Pattern (\d{4} \d{4} \d{4})│
│                      │                                                 │
│                      ├──► [Masking Engine]                             │
│                      │    - Replaces first 8 digits with 'X'           │
│                      │    - Output: "XXXX-XXXX-9012"                   │
│                      │    - Stored in Relational DB                    │
│                      │                                                 │
│                      └──► [Optional Protected Deduplication Token]     │
│                           - Keyed design only if formally approved     │
│                           - Key stored outside application/database    │
│                           - Pseudonymous data; access remains restricted│
│                                                                        │
│  * NO RAW AADHAAR PERSISTENCE IS A REQUIREMENT; CURRENT CODE MUST BE FIXED * │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Privacy and data governance:**  
   - Collect only data required for a stated purpose; provide clear notice, use an applicable legal basis, set retention/deletion rules, restrict access, and support grievances.
   - Never describe a plain or fixed-salt SHA-256 hash of Aadhaar as anonymous or irreversible. If deduplication is approved, use a protected keyed design, strict access limits, and a documented threat review.
   - The current prototype stores and returns raw Aadhaar values in its in-memory application records. Do not use real Aadhaar or other real applicant data in the demo until this is corrected.
2. **Data-at-Rest Encryption (planned):**  
   - Uploaded binary documents stored in S3/MinIO are encrypted using **AES-256-GCM** with per-file envelope encryption. Master keys are managed via hardware security modules (HSM) or cloud KMS.
3. **Data-in-Transit Encryption (planned):**  
   - All network calls enforce **TLS 1.3** cipher suites (`TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384`).
   - Administrative communication between State Nodal Officers and Ministry endpoints enforces mutual TLS (mTLS).
4. **Audit trail (planned control):**  
   - Every officer decision, manual review, and AI verification output creates an immutable cryptographic log entry:
   $$\text{Hash}_n = \text{SHA256}(\text{Hash}_{n-1} + \text{Timestamp} + \text{OfficerID} + \text{Action} + \text{TrustScore})$$
   - Hash chaining can make edits detectable; it does not by itself make a system immutable. Protect keys, storage permissions, backups, and audit access, and record the human decision and its reason.

## 6. PROPOSED SCALABILITY DESIGN & BENCHMARK PLAN

This section is a capacity-planning hypothesis only. Redis, Celery, object storage, autoscaling, and the stated 20-lakh workload are not implemented or benchmarked in the current repository. The figures below must not be presented as achieved capacity. Recalculate them from a documented workload model and repeatable load tests against the deployed build, including database, file storage, retries, and peak bursts. The current worker arithmetic also needs reconciliation before reuse.

### 6.1 The Deadline Day Challenge
Until an implementing agency provides real traffic data, use 20,00,000 applications over 72 hours and 250 to 500 requests per second only as hypothetical load-test scenarios, not as claims about NSP/PMS traffic.

### 6.2 Asynchronous De-Coupling via Task Queuing
An eventual production design may decouple image processing from application intake. The current prototype does not implement this queue:

```
[Student Browser]
       │  POST /api/verify-and-apply (Multipar/form-data)
       ▼
[NGINX Load Balancer] (SSL Termination, Rate Limit, Fast GZIP)
       │
       ▼
[FastAPI Gateway Instances] (worker count set by benchmark, not a fixed ratio)
       │
       ├──► 1. Save uploaded file buffer to S3 / Object Store (50ms)
       ├──► 2. Generate UUIDv4 Task ID (1ms)
       ├──► 3. Push Job Payload {task_id, file_uri, applicant_meta} to REDIS (2ms)
       │
       ▼
[HTTP 202 Accepted Response] (Returned to student in < 80 milliseconds)
       │
       ▼
[Client Polls / WebSocket] ──► Listens to /api/tasks/{task_id}/status every 2 seconds
```

### 6.3 Worker Pool Auto-Scaling Calculations
- Execution time of 8-stage AI Pipeline per document: $\approx 2.5 \text{ seconds}$ on 1 CPU Core.
- Single 8-Core Worker Node capacity: $8 \text{ cores} / 2.5 \text{s} = 3.2 \text{ verifications/second} \approx 11,520 \text{ docs/hour}$.
- To process peak burst of 300 RPS:
  $$\text{Required Worker Cores} = 300 \text{ RPS} \times 2.5 \text{s} = 750 \text{ Cores}$$
  - Distributed across **46 Kubernetes Pods** (each allocated 16 vCPUs, 32GB RAM).
  - Kubernetes Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) triggers scaling based on the **Redis Queue Length metric** (`redis_queue_unprocessed_jobs > 500`).
  - Do not claim a batch completion time from this simplified formula. Model storage, database, retries, queue bursts, and worker utilization, then measure the deployed system.

## 9. OPTIONAL BIOMETRIC RESEARCH: NOT A CORE SIH DEPENDENCY

Do not make face matching a required eligibility step or use it to auto-reject applicants. The current plan assumes access to an Aadhaar reference photo; do not assume that this data or API is available to the project. UIDAI describes face authentication as consent-based; any real integration needs an authorized route, explicit governance review, and a non-biometric fallback. For the SIH demo, use synthetic/sample images only and clearly label the result as experimental. See [UIDAI face authentication](https://uidai.gov.in/en/303-faqs/authentication.html).

## 10. PROPOSED RELATIONAL DATABASE SCHEMA (NOT YET CONNECTED)

This SQL is a starting point for implementation, not a live database schema. Keep scheme eligibility, human selection outcome, document-review signals, and payment readiness in separate fields. Never persist raw Aadhaar in application records. Any deduplication token needs an approved threat model and protected key management.

```sql
-- ====================================================================
-- TRIBALSETU ENTERPRISE RELATIONAL SCHEMA (POSTGRESQL 16)
-- ====================================================================

CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS "uuid-ossp";

-- 1. USERS & ROLES TABLE
CREATE TYPE user_role AS ENUM ('STUDENT', 'INO', 'DWO', 'SNO', 'MOTA_ADMIN', 'SUPERVISOR');

CREATE TABLE users (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    email VARCHAR(255) UNIQUE NOT NULL,
    phone VARCHAR(15) UNIQUE NOT NULL,
    password_hash VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    role user_role NOT NULL,
    aadhaar_hash CHAR(64) UNIQUE NOT NULL, -- Pseudonymous keyed token only if approved; key is never stored here
    full_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    is_active BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE,
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    updated_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 2. SCHEMES MASTER TABLE
CREATE TABLE schemes (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    scheme_code VARCHAR(50) UNIQUE NOT NULL, -- 'PMS-ST', 'NFST', 'NOS'
    name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    ministry VARCHAR(255) DEFAULT 'Ministry of Tribal Affairs',
    academic_year VARCHAR(20) NOT NULL,
    max_annual_income NUMERIC(12, 2) NOT NULL, -- e.g. 250000.00 for PMS-ST
    st_category_mandatory BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE,
    total_slots INT, -- NULL for entitlement schemes (PMS), 750 for NFST, 20 for NOS
    is_active BOOLEAN DEFAULT TRUE
);

-- 3. APPLICATIONS TABLE
CREATE TYPE review_priority AS ENUM ('ROUTINE', 'PRIORITY_REVIEW', 'URGENT_REVIEW', 'UNASSESSED');
CREATE TYPE application_status AS ENUM ('DRAFT', 'SUBMITTED', 'PENDING_VERIFICATION', 'UNDER_REVIEW', 'DEFICIENCY_ISSUED', 'RESUBMITTED', 'SELECTED', 'NOT_SELECTED', 'AWARD_ACTIVE', 'PAYMENT_PENDING', 'PAID', 'APPEAL_PENDING', 'CLOSED');

CREATE TABLE applications (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    application_number VARCHAR(50) UNIQUE NOT NULL, -- 'APP-2026-XXXXX'
    student_id UUID REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    scheme_id UUID REFERENCES schemes(id),
    institute_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    course_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    annual_income NUMERIC(12, 2) NOT NULL,
    claimed_caste VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,
    state VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,
    district VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,
    
    -- Eligibility, human selection outcome, and workflow status are separate
    eligibility_status VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING_VERIFICATION',
    selection_status VARCHAR(32) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'NOT_EVALUATED',
    review_priority review_priority NOT NULL DEFAULT 'UNASSESSED',
    status application_status NOT NULL DEFAULT 'DRAFT',
    
    -- Separate evidence; never a single auto-approval score
    qr_signature_status VARCHAR(32),
    ela_anomaly_score NUMERIC(5, 2),
    ocr_confidence NUMERIC(5, 2),
    identity_match_similarity NUMERIC(5, 2),
    dbt_readiness_status VARCHAR(32),
    
    -- Verification Artifacts
    audit_trail JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
    is_dbt_ready BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    submitted_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 4. VERIFIED DOCUMENTS TABLE
CREATE TYPE doc_type AS ENUM ('AADHAAR', 'CASTE_CERTIFICATE', 'INCOME_CERTIFICATE', 'DOMICILE', 'MARKSHEET');

CREATE TABLE documents (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    application_id UUID REFERENCES applications(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    document_type doc_type NOT NULL,
    s3_storage_uri VARCHAR(512) NOT NULL,
    s3_heatmap_uri VARCHAR(512),
    is_qr_present BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    qr_payload TEXT,
    ocr_extracted_fields JSONB DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb,
    is_tampered BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- 5. AUDIT & DISBURSEMENT LEDGER (Tamper-Proof)
CREATE TABLE disbursement_ledger (
    id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuid_generate_v4(),
    application_id UUID REFERENCES applications(id),
    sanctioned_amount NUMERIC(12, 2) NOT NULL,
    pfms_transaction_id VARCHAR(100) UNIQUE,
    dbt_account_last4 CHAR(4) NOT NULL,
    officer_id UUID REFERENCES users(id),
    disbursement_status VARCHAR(50) DEFAULT 'PROCESSING',
    previous_block_hash CHAR(64) NOT NULL,
    current_block_hash CHAR(64) NOT NULL,
    timestamp TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

CREATE INDEX idx_apps_status_review_priority ON applications(status, review_priority);
CREATE INDEX idx_apps_student ON applications(student_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_users_aadhaar ON users(aadhaar_hash);
```

## 12. CURRENT CODEBASE STATUS: IMPLEMENTED, SIMULATED & MISSING

This inventory describes the repository reviewed for this plan update. A source file or demo endpoint is not evidence of a production integration.

### Present as prototype code
- FastAPI application and student/officer web pages.
- Document processing modules for image preprocessing, ELA, QR decoding, OCR, tribe lookup, identity matching, and trust triage.
- Prototype modules for scheme rules, merit allocation, deficiency notices, fellowship records, biometrics, and an in-memory hash ledger.
- Sample applications and sample documents for demonstrations.

### Simulated or unsafe for real applicants
- Application records and several module records are held in process memory; they are lost on restart and are not a durable system of record.
- DBT/NPCI, PFMS, SMS/WhatsApp, and fellowship actions are simulated; no live government or communications gateway is connected.
- The current application endpoint accepts decision/score fields from the client, stores raw Aadhaar in memory, and can mark a client-submitted GREEN result as approved/paid. Application queues are not protected by implemented login and role authorization.
- CORS is permissive. The fixed salt and public hash endpoint must not be used as a secure Aadhaar deduplication design.
- The current dependency list does not provide the Redis/Celery/PostgreSQL architecture shown earlier in this document. The SQL schema is a proposal and is not wired into the running backend.

### Required before calling the system fully functional
- Server-side eligibility and workflow decisions; protected APIs with real authentication and role checks.
- Durable database and controlled document storage, backups, migrations, and data-retention behavior.
- Scheme rules sourced from current official guidelines, versioned by academic year, reviewed, and published through an authorized approval process.
- Applicant correction, deficiency, status tracking, grievance, and officer decision history.
- External integrations implemented and authorized, or visibly marked as simulated in the SIH demo.

The rule, merit, deficiency, fellowship, and biometric modules exist as prototypes. Their policy assumptions, persistence, access control, and edge cases still require review before they can be called complete.

## 13. PHASED PRODUCTION ROADMAP

### Phase 0 - Confirm policy and scope
Create a source-backed rule and workflow catalogue for PMS-ST, NFST, and NOS. For every academic year, record required documents, eligibility conditions, reviewer roles, selection method, communication events, and post-selection steps from the applicable official guideline. Keep NESTS/EMRS outside the core unless the team confirms it is in scope for this problem statement.

### Phase 1 - Secure and persistent core
Implement authentication and least-privilege authorization; move decisions to the backend; add durable persistence, migrations, document storage controls, backup/restore, and a versioned application state machine. Stop collecting or returning raw Aadhaar in normal application responses. Use synthetic test data until privacy and security controls have been reviewed.

### Phase 2 - Three scheme-specific workflows
Keep shared registration, application tracking, document upload, deficiency/resubmission, officer queues, audit history, and dashboards. Configure separate forms, checklists, eligibility rules, reviewer paths, selection methods, and post-selection handling for PMS-ST, NFST, and NOS. Do not apply a generic merit formula across schemes.

### Phase 3 - AI-assisted verification with human review
Use OCR to suggest extracted fields and highlight low-confidence values. Use QR verification only where issuer trust keys and signature validation are available. Use ELA and identity matching as review signals. Missing data goes to pending verification; uncertain or adverse signals go to an officer with evidence and an applicant correction route. Measure false positives and false negatives on representative data before publishing thresholds.

### Phase 4 - Communication and integration adapters
Build notification status, retries, delivery history, and applicant-facing messages. Keep DBT/PFMS, SMS, DigiLocker, or other external services behind adapters. In the demo, use clearly labelled simulators; use live services only after access, authorization, and operational requirements are satisfied.

### Phase 5 - Operational readiness
Add file validation, rate limits, secure configuration and key management, monitoring, audit review, accessibility and low-bandwidth support, disaster recovery, and repeatable security and load testing. Publish only measured performance results. A production launch also requires stakeholder approval and operational ownership.

## 14. HONEST SIH DEMONSTRATION CLAIMS

| Jury question | Accurate response |
|---|---|
| Can ELA prove a certificate is forged? | No. ELA can highlight compression anomalies. It is one review signal; scans and ordinary edits can create artifacts, and some manipulations may evade it. An officer reviews the evidence. |
| Does a missing QR mean fraud? | No. QR absence or an unsupported issuer goes to manual review. Signature verification is claimed only when a trusted issuer key and a valid verification path are implemented. |
| Is TribalSetu DPDP compliant today? | Do not claim that. The prototype needs privacy, security, retention, access, notice, and consent/legal-basis review before any real personal data is used. |
| Can it process 20 lakh applications or finish in 3.2 seconds? | Those are unverified design targets. State measured results only after repeatable end-to-end benchmarks on the deployed build. |
| Does AI approve or reject applications? | No. AI assists extraction and review prioritization. Eligibility and selection follow approved, scheme-specific rules; authorized officials record final decisions and reasons. |
| Are payments and notifications live? | Not in this prototype. DBT/PFMS and messaging responses are simulated until an authorized integration is connected. |

## 15. FULLY FUNCTIONAL SIH PROTOTYPE ACCEPTANCE PLAN

The SIH prototype is ready to demonstrate when all of the following are true:

1. One running platform offers PMS-ST, NFST, and NOS as separate scheme profiles with their own forms, document checklists, eligibility rules, reviewer paths, and selection/post-selection steps.
2. An applicant can create and resume a draft, submit documents, see a clear status, receive a deficiency with a reason, upload a correction, and track the next decision.
3. An authorized officer can review the relevant queue, see source documents and AI evidence, request correction or record a decision with a reason, and leave an auditable history.
4. Missing or uncertain evidence is never silently treated as a pass or fraud finding. AI scores cannot set final approval, rejection, or payment status.
5. Scheme rules are linked to an official source, academic year, version, and approver. NOS/NFST selection logic is not substituted with an unapproved generic weighting formula.
6. Data survives application restarts; applicant and officer endpoints enforce role-based access; demo data is synthetic; external integrations are visibly marked as simulated.
7. Dashboards report applications by scheme, state, workflow status, deficiency, and outcome, without exposing unnecessary personal data.
8. Before describing a capability as tested, secure, compliant, or scalable, the team has a repeatable test or review result supporting that exact claim.

This acceptance plan covers a credible SIH prototype. National deployment remains a separate milestone requiring approved scheme policies, authorized integrations, security/privacy review, operational support, and measured capacity.
